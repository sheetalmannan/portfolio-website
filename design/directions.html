Shared design language

These rules hold whichever palette you pick, so the decision is only about colour and type personality.

Grid & layout

  • 12 columns, 1200px max, 24px gutters, equal left/right edges.
  • Background: fine 48px grid with a crosshair dot every 4 cells (192px), so the dots line up with the columns.
  • Spacing scale 4 8 12 16 24 32 48 64 96 128.

Type hierarchy

  • Display face for the name and section titles only.
  • Readable sans for paragraphs: case studies are long, so no all-mono body.
  • Mono for labels, tags, dates and indices: 12px · 0.14em · UPPERCASE.
  • Pixel face appears only on the CRT and hologram.

Section labelling

  • Every section opens with 01 — ABOUT-style index + title.
  • The index sits on a circuit node that lights when the section is in view, so the labels are the circuit.
  • Nav mirrors the indices, so recruiters can jump straight to any section.

Cyberdeck language

  • Two-part body: hinged CRT display over a wider keyboard deck (≈1.15×).
  • Chunky radii, corner screws, side handle rails, vent slots, two knobs.
  • Exactly one accent key and one status LED in the brand accent.
  • Screen runs "SM-OS": boot lines, pixel avatar that blinks, blinking cursor.

Motion rules

  • One signal pulse travels the traces; nodes light on arrival.
  • Hovers 150–200ms ease-out; buttons press to 0.97.
  • No scroll-jacking or snapping, and no fade-in on every block.
  • Reduced motion: traces shown fully lit and static, avatar still.

Narrative link

  • Hero: pixel avatar lives on the CRT.
  • Down the page: traces carry the signal between sections.
  • Contact: traces converge into a processor, which projects the same avatar as a fuller hologram.

My recommendation: A · Phosphor & Putty

It fits the brief best. "Retro computing + modern minimal" is literally a putty-coloured machine on a clean editorial page, and the amber CRT gives the one glowing, memorable thing on the screen. A light, warm page keeps your case studies easy to read for recruiters, and it's visibly different from the dark neon portfolios most engineers ship.

Its one weakness, a hologram on a light page, becomes a narrative feature: the contact section switches to the CRT's dark glass colour (the "inside the machine" moment), so the hologram glows exactly where the story needs it.

If you'd like more drama overall, C is the strong alternative. Mixing is also possible (for example A's page with B's cyan hologram), but I'd keep to one accent family.
